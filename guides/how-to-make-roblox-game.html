How to Make Your Own Roblox Game

From downloading Studio to publishing live — no coding or experience needed.

⏱️ 15 min read ✅ Beginner Friendly 🆓 100% Free 🪟 Windows & Mac
Home › Guides › How to Make a Roblox Game

Every Roblox game — from massive titles with millions of visits to small obbies with a few hundred plays — was built using the same free tool: Roblox Studio. You can use it right now with just a Roblox account and a computer.

This guide covers everything: installing Studio, building your first world with free models, setting your game's age rating (required before it shows up in search), and publishing it live.

Before you start, try playing Rainbow Neon Fun Pizza Obby by Virality Games — a great example of what a beginner-built obby can look like.

📥 Part 1 — Download Roblox Studio

1

Check your PC or Mac meets the requirements

Most computers made after 2016 handle Studio fine on minimum specs. Worth a quick check before downloading.

Minimum vs recommended specs
Even on minimum specs Studio runs fine for small beginner projects. You'll only feel slowdowns on very large maps.
2

Go to create.roblox.com and click "Start Creating"

Head to create.roblox.com in your browser. Click the big "Start Creating" button to get to the Studio download page.

The Roblox Creator Hub — click "Start Creating"
3

Click "Download Studio"

Hit the blue "Download Studio" button. Run the installer when it downloads — it's quick and straightforward.

Click "Download Studio" — completely free
Only download from the official Roblox website (create.roblox.com). Third-party downloads can contain malware.
4

Open Roblox Studio from your Start menu

Press the Windows key and search "Roblox Studio" (Spotlight on Mac). Click it, sign in with your Roblox account, and you're in.

Search "Roblox Studio" and click to open

🔨 Part 2 — Build Your First Game

5

Click "Templates" and pick a starting point

Click Templates in the left sidebar. For your first game, Baseplate (empty world) or Classic Obby are the easiest options.

Classic Obby is perfect if you want to make a jumping game
Making an obby? Classic Obby already has platforms and a finish line — just swap things out and build on top of it.
6

Get comfortable with the Studio layout

The big window is your world. On the left is the Toolbox (free models). On the right is the Explorer (everything in your game). Click around — you can't break anything permanently.

Your world is in the middle, Toolbox on the left, Explorer on the right
7

Search for free models in the Toolbox

Make sure the Toolbox dropdown says "Models" and search for anything — trees, houses, obstacles, signs. Everything marked FREE costs nothing. Click one to drop it into your game.

Thousands of free models available — search for what fits your game
Search "house", "obstacle", "tree" — click any to insert it
Be selective with free models. Some contain hidden scripts. Stick to ones with clear names and lots of uses.
8

Press Play and test your game

Hit the green Play button to jump into your game as a player. Walk around, try to break things, and note anything that feels off. Press Stop to go back to editing.

Click Play to test — changes while playing are NOT saved
Walk through everything as if you're a first-time player
Always hit Stop before editing, or your changes will disappear when you exit play mode.

🔞 Part 3 — Set Your Age Rating (Required)

This is not optional. Without a content maturity label your game won't appear in search results or recommendations. It takes about two minutes.

9

Go to File → Experience Settings

In Studio, click File in the top-left menu bar, then click "Experience Settings".

File → Experience Settings
10

Click "Fill Out Questionnaire"

You'll see Content Maturity Label: Not Submitted. Click "Fill Out Questionnaire" — your browser will open the Creator Hub questionnaire.

Click "Fill Out Questionnaire"
11

Click "Start" and answer honestly

Click Start and work through the questions about your game's content. Be honest. A simple obby with no violence or mature themes will get a Minimal rating — the friendliest rating that reaches the widest audience.

Click Start and answer the questions truthfully
Don't mark your game as more family-friendly than it is. Roblox can remove games with misleading maturity labels, and it puts real kids in front of content they shouldn't see.
12

Review your rating and click "Submit"

You'll see a preview of your content label. If it looks right, click Submit. Then click Settings in the left sidebar.

Review and hit Submit
Success — now click Settings in the left sidebar

🚀 Part 4 — Go Live

13

Set your game to Public in the Creator Hub

In the Creator Hub Settings page, scroll to Privacy and switch it from Private to Public. Save. Your game is now live and searchable.

Switch Privacy to Public and save — you're live!
14

Publish future updates with File → Publish to Roblox

Any time you update your game in Studio, use File → Publish to Roblox (Alt+P) to push changes live. Fill in a descriptive name and description — both affect discoverability.

File → Publish to Roblox pushes your latest changes live
Use a clear, searchable name — include a genre word like "obby" or "tycoon"
Short and descriptive beats vague every time: "Pizza Obby", "Rainbow Parkour", "Neon Jump Challenge" all work better than "My Game".

🎉 You Did It — What's Next?

Upload a thumbnail — go to your Creator Hub page and upload a colourful image. Thumbnails are the biggest factor in whether someone clicks your game.

Share the link — post it on Roblox group walls, Discord servers, Reddit (r/roblox), or TikTok. Visits don't come automatically at first.

Learn basic Lua scripting — even one script (a coin to collect, a timer, a colour-changing part) makes your game feel alive.

Study games you enjoy — Rainbow Pizza Obby shows how a simple concept with good visuals can be genuinely fun. Play it, notice what works, apply it to your own game.
