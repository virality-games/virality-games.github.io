What Does "Projected" Mean on Rolimons?

The warning tag that tells you an item's RAP is a lie — and what to do about it.

⏱️ 5 min read 🏷️ Trading ⚠️ Read Before Trading
Home › Guides › What Does Projected Mean on Rolimons
⚡ Short answer

A projected item has been manipulated — someone sold copies at an artificially high price to inflate its RAP. The item looks valuable on paper but is actually worth a fraction of what Rolimons shows. If you have one and you're not experienced, get rid of it fast.

What Is a Projected Item?

When you look up a Roblox limited on Rolimons, you'll usually see three numbers: Price (what it's listed for on the catalog), RAP (Recent Average Price — the average of the last 10 sales), and Value (Rolimons' estimated fair trade value).

Normally these three numbers are reasonably close to each other. On a projected item, RAP is massively higher than value — sometimes by 10x, 50x, or even 100x. This is not a mistake. It's the result of someone deliberately selling copies of that item at an inflated price to spike the RAP.

Projected items on Roblox are limiteds that have been intentionally sold at high prices to artificially inflate their RAP. This makes them appear far more valuable than they actually are to traders who don't understand what they're looking at.

🔍 How to Spot the Projected Tag

Rolimons flags projected items with a yellow warning triangle icon. If you see this on an item, treat it as a red flag before you trade for it.

⚠️ That yellow triangle = projected

The warning icon appears on the item card in Rolimons. Any time you see it, the RAP is not a reliable indicator of what the item is actually worth in trades. Look at the Value instead — that's the real number.

📉 A Real Example: The Beach House Fedora

The Beach House Fedora is one of the clearest examples of a projected item. At a glance it looks like a two-million Robux item. It's not.

🎩 Beach House Fedora — What the Numbers Actually Mean
Price (catalog listing) 1,840,000 R$
RAP (recent average price) 2,565,661 R$
Value (what it actually trades for) ~20,000–25,000 R$
RAP vs real value gap ~128× overinflated

The RAP is over 2.5 million. The item actually trades for around 20,000. That gap exists because someone sold copies at an artificially high price specifically to inflate the RAP number — making the item look valuable to inexperienced traders who might accept it in a trade.

Item projections typically don't last very long. Once the projection happens, copies start selling far below the inflated RAP, which drags the RAP back down with every sale. The longer you hold a projected item, the lower its RAP gets — and the harder it becomes to trade away.

If someone offers you a projected item in a trade, they are almost certainly trying to take advantage of you. The item looks valuable because of its RAP — but experienced traders know the RAP is fake and won't accept it at that value.

🚨 What To Do If You Have a Projected Item

1

Don't panic, but act quickly

The RAP on a projected item decays with every sale. The sooner you trade it away, the higher the RAP still is — which gives you a better chance of getting something reasonable in return before traders wise up.

2

Never trade it at RAP value

Don't expect to get 2.5 million worth of items for your Beach House Fedora. Experienced traders will immediately recognize the projected tag and reject the trade. Be honest about what it's worth — around its Rolimons Value, not its RAP.

3

Look for newer or less informed traders

This isn't a great long-term strategy, but if you need to move a projected item quickly, trade ads on Rolimons can help you find someone willing to take it. Be upfront — knowingly deceiving another trader creates a bad reputation fast in the Roblox trading community.

4

If you're experienced — hold and watch

Some traders intentionally pick up projected items cheap and flip them while the RAP is still elevated. This is genuinely risky and requires knowing exactly what you're doing. If you're asking what "projected" means, you're probably not at that level yet — and that's completely fine.

Always check Rolimons Value, not RAP, when evaluating a trade. If the Value is way lower than the RAP and there's a warning triangle on the item, walk away unless you know what you're doing.
